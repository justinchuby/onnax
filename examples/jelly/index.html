THE INTERESTING PART

One energy.
Two derivatives.

The sheet is 36 moving points joined by 110 springs. Let x collect their positions. Stretching or compressing a spring stores energy:

Espring(x) = ½k ∑(i,j) (ℓij(x) − ℓij0)²

Here k is stiffness, ℓ is a smoothed spring length, and ℓ⁰ is its rest length. Add gravity and the pointer's spring to get physical energy U(x). Differentiating this model gives forces and guides the next simulation step.

*Illustrative units; a coarse spring sheet, not a calibrated material model.

01

Choose the next position Φ(x)

Each timestep minimizes physical energy plus inertia and damping terms. Inertia keeps the sheet moving; damping resists motion. Pinned points stay fixed.

Differentiate once g = ∇Φ(x)

The gradient tells the solver how to lower the step objective; a small gradient means the step is nearly solved. For the arrows, −∇U points toward decreasing physical energy: this is force.

03

Differentiate again Hw = jvp(grad(Φ), w)

For a trial direction w, Hw predicts how the gradient changes as points move. Newton-CG uses these products to choose a better update for stiff springs, without building a full Hessian matrix. A line search checks that each accepted update lowers Φ.

The timestep equation & ONNX graphs

Given current position xt and velocity vt, solve for xt+1 by minimizing:

Φ(x) = U(x) + m/(2h²) ‖x − xt − h vt‖² + mc/(2h) ‖x − xt‖²

m is mass per point, h is the timestep, and c is the damping setting. The quadratic terms encode inertia and viscous damping. Then vt+1 = (xt+1 − xt)/h. This is an implicit step: forces depend on the position being solved for.

Newton-CG approximately solves (H + λI)p = −g, with H = ∇²Φ and a positive regularizer λ, then tries x + αp. Conjugate gradient only needs products Hw, generated by onnax.jvp(onnax.grad(...)). The residual above measures ‖∇Φ‖ over free coordinates; smaller means closer to the step's equilibrium.

Execution details & limitations

Waiting for a WebGPU adapter.

ONNX Runtime runs the FLOAT32 physics graphs on WebGPU (opset 22), with CPU fallback disabled. Canvas 2D draws the results. Timings include graph execution and readback; slower devices advance simulation time more slowly. If a solve fails, the demo pauses and keeps the last valid state.
